# whoodeo

whoodeo upscales low-quality video 2×. A vae2 codes master frames into a small latent; latent_up maps three low frames (previous, current, next) to that latent of the middle master, conditioned on the source codec, and the frozen vae2 decoder turns it into the picture. Training runs in PyTorch (`whoodeo-train`), `whoodeo-apply` runs a checkpoint on a video, and the macOS app (`macos/Whoodeo`) runs latent_up through MLX. Older models (shift, pyramid, vote, EDSR, SwinIR, the codebook model, the Gaussian vae, one-frame latent_up, the Core ML latent_up export) are in git history.

# The vae2 model

`arch: vae2` reconstructs a master frame. It does not read a low variant. The encoder goes down by stride-2 convolutions to `latent` channels at 1/`factor` of the frame (a power of two from 2 to 16), and the decoder starts from that latent alone, with no skip from the encoder. The encoder gives μ only: no sample and no KL (older checkpoints carry `posterior: fixed` in their architecture, which still loads). The layers are 3×3 convolutions, an activation, additions, and nearest-neighbor doubling. There is no normalization and no attention. `activation` under `model:` is `silu` (the default, and what a checkpoint without the key means) or `relu`; it is part of the saved architecture, so loading, resuming, and the exports pick it up, and a resume refuses the other one. `channels` is the width at full resolution. By default the width doubles at half resolution and is four times `channels` from a quarter down, so `channels: 64` runs 64, 128, 256, 256 from the frame to the latent. Full resolution has one residual block, each level below it two. `blocks` is the number of residual blocks at the latent, with dilations 1, 2, and 4 in turn. Encoder and decoder mirror each other. The layout can be set apart: `encoder_blocks` and `decoder_blocks` name the residual count per level above the latent (full resolution first), and `widths` names a multiple of `channels` per level including the latent; `configs/vae2g-spark.yaml` uses `encoder_blocks: [1, 2, 2]`, `decoder_blocks: [2, 3, 2]`, `widths: [1, 2, 4, 4]`. The last convolution of every residual branch starts at zero. The default layout has 22.2M parameters, and the decoder costs about 1.2 TFLOP for a 720p frame.

A side that `factor` does not divide is padded on the bottom and the right, and the reconstruction is cut back to the original size. `crop` is optional. Left out, each sample is the whole master frame, and the batch stays inside one master size. Set to a positive multiple of `factor`, every sample is that window. A training window is placed at random. A validation window is the center, and it stays there, so `recon-<step>.png` can be compared across steps. `master.png` is written once. TensorBoard has `train_latent/mu_mean` and `mu_std` for the batch code and the same under `val_latent/` for the held-out frames, with the histogram `val_latent/mu` and a per-channel table `val_latent/channels`.

`decode(latent, clamp=True)` limits the frame to [0, 1]. Training compares the unclamped output. Each validation stores per-channel `latent_shift` and `latent_scale` in the model: (μ − shift) × scale has mean 0 and std 1 in each channel. A model that works on latents should see that scaled code and hand z / scale + shift back to the decoder. TensorBoard logs the mean scale as `val_latent/scale`. The mean shift is `val_latent/mu_mean`.

The `color` loss term is L1 between average-pooled frames, the chroma channels weighted twice: it pools in linear light and compares Oklab L, a, b. `pool` is the pooling factor, 8 by default, and part of the loss recipe. The term is for color drift in flat areas. The current vae2 weights are pixel 1.0, VGG `relu2_2` 0.230 / `relu3_4` 0.155, and color 5.5 in Oklab with pool 16.

Two terms can take the place of pixel and color. `oklab` is L1 in Oklab at full resolution: gamma sRGB to linear light to LMS, the cube root, and L, a, b, each weighed by `channels: [L, a, b]` (default `[1, 3, 3]`; on vae2d mean |ΔL| is about 3.2 times |Δa| or |Δb|, so at 3 each channel weighs about the same). Below (6/29)³ in LMS the cube root becomes its tangent line, as in CIE L\*a\*b\*, so the slope near black stays at 7.8 and an overshoot below black keeps a gradient. `ms_ssim` is 1 − MS-SSIM (Wang 2003: Gaussian window 11, σ 1.5, the five standard scale weights) on Oklab L, `scales` 1 to 5 (default 5); scales whose shorter side falls below the window are dropped and the rest renormalized, so the pooled `scale_eq` frames work too. Both log `train_raw/`, `val_raw/` and the weighted `train/`, `val/` values like the other terms. `configs/vae2g-spark.yaml` uses oklab 4.5 with `channels: [1, 1, 1]` (raw 0.00313 on vae2d, the mean of the channels) and ms_ssim 1 beside the VGG weights, calibrated on 32 val frames so that they hold the share against VGG that pixel + color held: 23.3% against 23.4% on vae2d step 15755, 85% against 86% at step 0, but 50% against 41% at vae2e step 250, where 1 − MS-SSIM is still large.

`latent_noise: {tau: ...}` adds per-channel-relative noise to the latent in training. Each frame draws s from |N(0, τ²)| and gets s × std_c × N(0, 1), where std_c is a running std of μ per channel. It sits outside the loss recipe, so a resume may change it; `tau: 0` turns it off for a clean final fine-tune. Validation still decodes μ.

Two more terms stand beside the objective, in the step loss only: `mu_l2` is `weight` times mean(μ²), against scale drift, and `scale_eq` picks one of its `factors` (for example `[2, 4]`) each step, decodes the average-pooled latent, and compares it with the pooled frame under the same pixel, VGG, color, oklab, and ms_ssim weights.

`faces: {weight, score, expand, feather}` (vae2 and latent_up) weighs the image terms up inside faces. OpenCV YuNet (`cv2.FaceDetectorYN`, the `opencv-python-headless` dependency) runs on the CPU on every frame exactly as the batch hands it to the model, after any crop: in the loader thread for training, on the main thread for validation, on the frame scaled to at most 640 pixels on its longer side, about 6 ms a frame on the Spark. There are no cached boxes. The model file `face_detection_yunet_2023mar.onnx` (opencv_zoo, MIT) sits in `~/.cache/whoodeo/` (`$XDG_CACHE_HOME/whoodeo`); a missing file is downloaded on first use and its SHA-256 checked. Boxes at `score` (default 0.6) or above grow by `expand` (1.2) about their center; the soft mask is that box blurred by a Gaussian of `feather` (0.1) times its shorter side, the maximum where faces overlap. The weight map is 1 + (`weight` − 1) × mask, divided by its mean per frame: every frame keeps a mean weight of 1, so the loss scale and the term balance stay those of the recipe, a frame without faces counts as before, and in a frame with faces the rest gives up what the faces gain. `oklab` multiplies its L1 by the map, `ms_ssim` takes the weighted mean of the SSIM and contrast-structure maps at each scale (the map pooled with the frames), and `vgg` multiplies each layer's L1 by the map area-pooled to that layer; `pixel`, `color`, `scale_eq`, and `mu_l2` stay unweighted. Validation weighs its objective the same way and logs `val/psnr_face` and `val/psnr_nonface`, the PSNR pooled over all face pixels (mask ≥ 0.5) and all others, beside `train_raw/face_fraction` and `val_raw/face_fraction`. The live page draws the boxes on `recon` and `master`. For latent_up see its section below. `faces` stays outside the loss recipe, like `latent_noise`; weight 1 or no `faces:` runs no detector at all.

Each vae2 validation also logs `val_noise/psnr_{0.1,0.3,0.5}` (PSNR of decode(μ + s × std_c × N(0, 1)) with a fixed seed) and `val_noise/scale_eq_psnr` (decode of the 2×2 pooled μ against the 2×2 pooled frame). The per-channel SNR sits in the `val_latent/channels` table.

`configs/vae2g-spark.yaml` is the current recipe, that of the vae2 train-20261119-180935 under the current latent_up: the layout above with 16 latent channels and relu, latent noise with tau 0.2, oklab 4.5 and ms_ssim 1 beside VGG, `mu_l2` at 5e-4, `scale_eq` at 0.25, and face weighting, at batch 6 on the DGX Spark. Its comments say where each choice comes from, through the earlier recipes vae2c to vae2f (in git history). A 40-step smoke run on the Spark (21.10.2026) took 2.3–2.6 s/step at 480p and 5.2–5.9 s at 720p, left about 60 GB RAM available, and went from val/psnr 5.9 to 19.0. The loader reads the frames of a training batch on 8 threads at once (`_READ_THREADS` in `train.py`), because each read opens its video, seeks, and closes it again: over the VPN mount on spark-03 (19.11.2026) a random master frame cost about 0.34 s and 0.7 MB on the wire, and a batch of 6 took 1.83 s read one by one against 0.57 s in parallel.

The user config `~/.config/whoodeo/config.yaml` (`$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/whoodeo`) names the machine's directories and the live page, every key optional: `data`, the folder with `orig/`, `low/` and `catalog.json`; `runs`, the folder of the runs and `latest`; `events`, a folder for the TensorBoard events, one subfolder per run name, which otherwise stay in the run directory; and `live`, the `host:port` of the live page of `whoodeo-train` and `whoodeo-apply` (default `127.0.0.1:8765`, `0.0.0.0:8765` serves it to the network, port 0 picks a free one). On a network mount each TensorBoard event is its own append, which made each step wait about 5 s over the VPN mount on spark-03, so there `events` points to the local disk. spark-03:

```
data: /mnt/home-storinator-trem/whoodeo/train
runs: /mnt/home-storinator-trem/whoodeo/runs
events: ~/whoodeo-events
live: 0.0.0.0:8765
```

A vae2 may add a `gan` term with the U-Net discriminator. Its weight is not a share of the objective. The generator term is the latent diffusion adaptive weight, the gradient norm of the objective over that of the GAN loss at the last decoder convolution, times `weight`. The discriminator trains from the first step. The generator term joins at `start`. The validation objective leaves the GAN out. `freeze_encoder: true` trains the decoder alone. `configs/vae2-gan.yaml` combines the two as a fine-tune of a vae2 run:

```
uv run whoodeo-train configs/vae2-gan.yaml --model <run>/latest
```

`whoodeo-coreml` turns a vae2 checkpoint into Core ML packages for the VAE playground (below). The default part is the decoder: an fp16 latent in, the clamped fp16 frame out. The input takes the latent sizes 60×107, 90×160, and 135×240, which are 480p, 720p, and 1080p at factor 8. `--latent 90x160` gives one fixed size instead. The metadata carries the per-channel latent shift and scale.
`--part encoder` gives the encoder instead (the frame in, sides 8× the latent sizes, μ out, the same metadata), and `--part pair` writes both. Without `-o` the packages go to `~/Library/Application Support/Whoodeo/VAE`. It needs coremltools in the venv (`uv pip install --python .venv/bin/python 'coremltools==9.1.dev1'`). `--conv-scale k` (16 by default, 1 turns it off) multiplies each convolution's weight and bias by k and puts a separate multiply by 1/k behind it, with coremltools' `fuse_conv_scale` pass off so the multiply stays apart. Residual convolutions that start at zero would otherwise make very small products. It costs nothing measurable. `activation: relu` emits a plain relu; silu is approximated when Core ML fuses it.

## The VAE playground

The macOS app has a VAE playground (Darstellung → VAE-Playground, ⇧⌘L), its own window. It loads the newest encoder and decoder pair from `~/Library/Application Support/Whoodeo/VAE`; the model list does not look there. To fill it on the Mac:

    .venv/bin/whoodeo-coreml /Volumes/home/whoodeo/runs/<vae2 run>/checkpoints/<step> --part pair

Pictures A and B are a frame of a film (the opened films, or any file) or a picture file, filled and cropped to the working size (480p, 720p, or 1080p, the decoder's latent sizes). Everything works on the normalized latent, (μ - shift) · scale, so 1 means one standard deviation of that channel. Four panes: original A, its roundtrip with PSNR, the pixel blend of A and B at the same mix (or original B, or the difference ×4 without B), and the result with PSNR against A and against the pixel blend. Below them the latent channels as gray tiles, −3σ black and +3σ white. The controls: linear or spherical blend from A to B (and a pendulum); Gaussian noise up to σ 2, fine or blotchy, on all channels or one, with a seed and a smooth wobble; per channel mute, solo, invert, factor, offset, take it from B, and swap two channels; all channels times a factor; mirror and shift the latent; average it in blocks; quantize it to a few bits over ±3σ; stretch it to a larger latent size before decoding (nearest or bilinear); and repeated encode–decode roundtrips to see drift. Encoder and decoder run on the GPU, the decoder in about 100 ms at 720p. `Whoodeo.app/Contents/MacOS/Whoodeo --vae-roundtrip picture-or-video` prints the roundtrip PSNR of the newest pair at each size.

# The latent_up model

`arch: latent_up` is a 2× upscaler that predicts the code of a frozen vae2 instead of pixels. The low frame is h × w and its master 2h × 2w. A vae2 at factor 8 codes the master at h/4 × w/4, so the network maps the low RGB frame to that grid: the normalized code z = (μ − shift) × scale, which has mean 0 and std 1 per channel. The frozen vae2 decoder turns z / scale + shift into the 2× picture. A side that 4 does not divide is padded like vae2 pads, and the picture is cut back to 2h × 2w.

`vae` under `model:` names the vae2, a run directory (its latest checkpoint, as `--model` reads it), a checkpoint, or a model.pt. The config stores the checkpoint directory it resolved to, so a `--resume` decodes with the same vae2 and refuses another. The vae2 stays in eval mode, its weights get no gradient, and there is no latent noise. `latent` must match its channels. The network has the vae2 encoder's building blocks: a 3×3 stem, residual blocks of conv and activation (`activation`, `silu` or `relu` as in vae2, independent of the vae2's) whose last convolution starts at zero, a stride-2 convolution to each coarser level, `blocks` residual blocks at 1/4 with dilations 1, 2, 4 in turn, and a 3×3 head. `channels` is the width at the low resolution, `widths` the multiples at low, 1/2, and 1/4, `encoder_blocks` the residual count at low and at 1/2.

The `latent` loss term is L1 between the predicted z and the vae2 code of the master, encoded in the same step without gradient. The image terms (`pixel`, `vgg`, `color`, `oklab`, `ms_ssim`) compare the decoded picture with the master, and their gradient runs through the frozen decoder into z. Every term is optional. `train_raw/latent` and `val_raw/latent` are logged even when the term is left out. The first validation of a process logs three references on the same frames: `val_ref/bicubic_psnr`, `val_ref/bilinear_psnr`, and `val_ref/vae_psnr`, the vae2 roundtrip decode(encode(master)), which is the ceiling of this decoder. The live page shows `low` (bicubic 2×), `upscale`, and `master`; each validation frame folder also gets `vae.png`, that roundtrip. With `faces:` YuNet runs on the master of each pair (in the loader thread for training, on the main thread for validation), and the weight map weighs `oklab`, `ms_ssim`, and `vgg` exactly as for the vae2. The `latent` L1 is weighted too: the map is area-pooled by the vae2 factor 8 onto the latent grid (a last partial cell averages the pixels it has, 852 → 107) and divided again by its mean per frame. `train_raw/latent` and `val_raw/latent` are then the face-weighted L1. Validation adds `val/psnr_face`, `val/psnr_nonface`, and `val_raw/face_fraction`, and the first one also `val_ref/vae_psnr_face` and `val_ref/vae_psnr_nonface`, the roundtrip ceiling on the same pixels. The live page draws the boxes on `upscale` and `master`. `configs/latent-up-3f-128-falign-spark.yaml` is the current run, on vae2g, with the vae2g image recipe (oklab 4.5, ms_ssim 1, VGG 0.230 / 0.155) beside latent 1.0, and the vae2g faces. `whoodeo-apply` takes a latent_up checkpoint, and the MLX port turns one into the app's model, see below.

## The codec condition (FiLM)

`condition: codec` under `model:` tells latent_up which codec made the low frame. The vector has 11 slots: one-hot h264, mpeg4, mpeg2 (mpeg2 has no variants yet), then 8 reserve slots that stay 0, so the kinds of deinterlacing, a strength, or blur can come later without a new shape and without fiddling with checkpoints. There is no unknown slot: unknown is all codec slots 0, and the MLP's bias makes that its own learned default. A small MLP (linear 11 → 64, the model's activation, linear → γ and β) gives a scale γ and a shift β per channel of every residual block (2 + 4 + 12 blocks, 64/128/256 channels), and each block's output becomes x · (1 + γ) + β. The last MLP layer starts at zero, so a fresh FiLM is exactly the network without it: a latent_up checkpoint without condition loads into it with `--model` (only the FiLM weights are new), and its step 0 is the loaded network. The encoder keeps its parameter names. A zero input gets no gradient, so the reserve slots learn nothing until they are used; a fine-tune that fills them starts them from their initial weights, which is what a fresh column would do.

The codec comes from the low variant's name, the part before the first `-`: `x264-…` is h264, `mpeg4-…` mpeg4, `mpeg2-…` mpeg2, anything else unknown. The training log prints that mapping and how many films have each codec. `codec_unknown` (0.2 in the config) sets that share of the training frames to unknown, on its own random generator, so the batches are the ones the same seed draws without a condition; the loader and its variant draw are unchanged. The step line ends in `codecs h264/mpeg4/mpeg2/unknown` frame counts of the batch as fed, and `train_codec/` holds the share of each over the run so far. Validation always passes the true codec and also decodes every frame with the codec unknown: `val/psnr_unknown`, and `val_codec/psnr_<codec>` with `val_codec/frames_<codec>` per codec.

The app's MLX model keeps the FiLM MLP in its graph, a multiply and an add behind each block, so the app sets the vector per film and later reserve slots (the kind of deinterlacing, a strength, blur) need no new export; its name ends in `-cond`. `whoodeo-apply --codec` sets the same vector on a checkpoint. The mpeg2 slot has seen no training frame yet (make-low has no mpeg2 variants), so its first FiLM column is still the random initial one, and the trained last layer turns that into a modulation that has nothing to do with MPEG-2: at step 2000 an mpeg2-conditioned frame was 21 dB away from the other codecs on the same frame, where h264, mpeg4, and unknown were 44–52 dB apart. Until mpeg2 has been trained, the app's automatic choice feeds MPEG-2 films the unknown vector (all zero); the menu can still pick MPEG-2 on purpose.

## Three input frames

`in_frames: 3` under `model:` gives latent_up the previous, middle, and next low frame, stacked on the channels in that order (9 channels). 3 is the default and what the MLX port and the app run. The loader reads them with one seek and one decode per sample, and at the clip's edges the middle frame stands in for the missing neighbor. Each frame runs through the encoder's stem and the two residual blocks at low resolution (128 channels at width 128, with FiLM when there is a condition), so every frame has its features at full low resolution. `FeatureAlign` then brings the neighbors onto the middle frame inside the model: a frozen official SPyNet computes the optical flow from the middle low frame to the previous one and to the next one (two runs, no symmetry assumed: occlusions and SPyNet itself make the two directions differ), the neighbor features are warped along it (bilinear, border, as `grid_sample` in SPyNet), and a fusion reads the middle features, both warped neighbor features, and one warp-error channel per neighbor (the mean absolute difference between the warped neighbor low frame and the middle low frame, over RGB): a 3×3 convolution (3 × 128 + 2 → 128), the activation, and a 3×3 convolution (128 → 128), added onto the middle features. From the stride-2 convolution to 1/2 on there is one stream: 4 blocks at 1/2, 12 at 1/4, the head. The fusion has PyTorch's default start. SPyNet's weights are part of the model and its checkpoint but never train (requires_grad false, outside the optimizer and the weight average); a fresh run loads the official weights from `spynet_weights` at the top of the config, `--resume` and `--model` take them from the checkpoint. Training, `whoodeo-apply`, and the MLX port feed the raw 9 channels; nothing aligns outside the model. A scene cut shows as a large warp error, which the fusion can learn to read. `--model` refuses a checkpoint with other `in_frames`, and the three-frame latent_up with the old `FrameMix` at 1/4 (and the pixel alignment before the generator, `align: spynet`) no longer loads, nor does the one-frame latent_up; all are in git history. The architecture key holds `in_frames`, so `--resume` refuses a mismatch.

`scene_cut` (0 by default; 0.1 in the config) is the chance per neighbor and training frame that the neighbor is swapped for the middle frame of another frame of the batch, which has the same size and is mostly another film: a stand-in for a scene cut, so the network learns to ignore a neighbor that does not belong. The swap happens on the raw low frames in the loader, before the model's alignment. It is drawn on its own generator (seed + 2), so the batches stay those of the seed; validation reads the true neighbors. The step line ends in `cuts n`, the swaps of the batch (`train_raw/scene_cuts`). `train_neighbor/weight` and `train_neighbor/grad` are the mean absolute kernel of the fusion's last layer and its gradient over the head's, and `val_neighbor/image` (also `neighbors` on the validation line) is the mean absolute change of the validation frames when both neighbors are replaced by the middle frame.

`whoodeo-apply` runs a checkpoint in PyTorch and feeds it the window around each frame. The app runs it through MLX (below), reads the frame count, and feeds the window, previous, current, next, with the same edge repeat, in the viewer, the frame cache, the export, `--upscale`, and `--pace`; the sidebar shows it as "latent_up 12×128 · 3 Bilder".

## Weight average (ema)

`ema: d` at the top of a config (any arch; off when left out) keeps a moving average of the generator weights beside the trained ones: after each optimizer step every weight's average moves by (1 − d) × (weight − average), with d = min(decay, (1 + n) / (10 + n)) after n updates so a fresh average follows quickly. Validation, its images, the plateau, and model.pt use the average; the optimizer and the live preview of a step use the trained weights. train.pt also holds the trained weights (`weights`) and the update count (`ema`), so `--resume` continues both; a resume without `ema` in the file continues the trained weights, one with `ema` from a checkpoint without starts the average from the loaded weights, and `--model` starts it from the loaded model.pt. model.pt keeps its format, so `whoodeo-apply` and the MLX port read it as before. The reason: in the FiLM run the validation PSNR dropped by about 1 dB every few validations, a global brightness offset of 2 to 3 of 255 from the step-to-step jitter of the weights. 0.999 averages over about the last 1000 steps.

## whoodeo-apply and the macOS app

`whoodeo-apply` runs latent_up on a video in PyTorch. A latent_up checkpoint loads the vae2 its config names (a path under `/mnt/home-storinator-trem` is looked up under `/Volumes/home`, then `~/storinator/home`, where the Mac mounts the same share; `--vae` overrides it) and runs the path validation takes, code, z / scale + shift, decode, clamp, crop to 2h × 2w, on MPS in fp32. The end of the run prints the median model time per frame. On the Mac, without `uv run`, so the training dependencies (OpenCV) need not be in the venv:

    .venv/bin/whoodeo-apply /Volumes/home/whoodeo/runs/<run>/checkpoints/<step> low.mkv --out up.mkv

The macOS app (`macos/Whoodeo`, open `Whoodeo.xcodeproj`, or `xcodebuild -scheme Whoodeo -configuration Release`) lists the `.mlxmodel` models in `~/Library/Application Support/Whoodeo/Models`, newest first, and names each by width, frame count, step, and run ("latent_up 12×128 · 3 Bilder", "Schritt 8691 · Lauf 24.11. 10:04"). Models get there through the MLX port (below); there is no import dialog. Every model runs on the GPU, at any size from 4×4 to 8192×8192; padding and the crop to 2h × 2w happen inside the model. Frame caches belong to one model: when a model leaves the folder, whether deleted in the app or outside it, the app removes its caches, and it checks again at every start. Without the window, `Whoodeo.app/Contents/MacOS/Whoodeo --check model.mlxmodel in.f32 out.f32` runs one tensor file.

### The codec menu

A latent_up with FiLM comes as one model, `…-step<step>-3f-hd-align-cond.mlxmodel`, with the condition vector as a second input. The sidebar shows one line per checkpoint ("… · Codec-Bedingung"). On opening a film the app reads the codec of its picture stream from FFmpeg: h264 is h264, MPEG-4 Part 2 (DivX, XviD) and the Microsoft MPEG-4 of DivX 3 are mpeg4, MPEG-1 and MPEG-2 are mpeg2, anything else is unknown. The Codec menu (step 2 of the pipeline panel, below) is "Automatisch (<codec of the film>)" by default, or h264, MPEG-4, MPEG-2, unbekannt fixed. The app then feeds that codec's vector into the same model; unknown is all zero. Automatic MPEG-2 falls back to unknown until that slot has been trained; picking MPEG-2 in the menu still sets the mpeg2 slot. Changing the codec or opening a film of another codec keeps the model and switches the vector and the frame cache. The window title and the line under the picture name the active codec, and the log (category `model` of `meins.whoodeo`) records the film's codec, the choice, and the model. For a model without FiLM the menu is greyed out. During an export the codec stays.

The frame cache of a `-cond` model belongs to the model (path, size, and date of its weights) and the condition in force, so h264 and mpeg4 frames of the same film never mix, and switching back finds the frames of that codec again. Moving a FiLM line to the trash removes its model and its caches. The AppleScript properties `source codec` (auto, h264, mpeg4, mpeg2, unknown) and `active codec` do the same as the menu. Without the window, `Whoodeo --upscale <model> <film> <index> [out.f32] [--source-codec auto|h264|…]` scales one frame through the cache the way the window does: it feeds the given codec, prints the film's codec, the model, and the cache folder, and says whether the frame came from the cache. `--export` takes `--source-codec` too.

### The pipeline panel

The panel on the right (toolbar button "Pipeline") shows what happens to a film on its way to the screen, top to bottom, and its settings belong to each film (`films.json`):

1. **Quelle.** "Doppelte Bilder" drops the frame that a transcode repeated in every cycle: 6er-Takt for 25 fps blown up to 30 (one copy in six), 5er-Takt for 24 → 30, or Automatisch. The app measures once how much every frame differs from the one before (8-bit luma on a coarse grid, decoded in parallel stretches; 53,816 frames at 320×240 take about a second, 107,030 at 720p about 26 s) and keeps the numbers in the film's cache folder (`decimate.bin`), so switching or reopening is instant. Per cycle the pair that differs least is the copy when it differs at most half as much as the next-closest pair and that pair shows motion; still or unclear stretches keep the position of the last clear cycle, so a cut that shifts the cadence costs at most one cycle. Automatisch takes a cycle length only when the copy keeps its position from cycle to cycle in most cycles. The rest of the app then sees the kept frames as the film: the count, a reduced rate (30 → 25 fps), evenly spaced times over the same length (so an export keeps the sound in sync), and real neighbors in the three-frame window. A decimated film has its own frame caches (suffix `-dec6` or `-dec5`), so the plain frames stay. `Whoodeo --decimate <film> [auto|cycle6|cycle5]` runs the same scan from the command line and prints the cycle, the frame count, and where the cadence shifts.
2. **Upscaler.** The codec menu and the model in use.
3. **Farbe & Licht.** Apple's auto-enhance chain as editable values, applied after the upscale in Apple's order: `CIFaceBalance` (Wärme and Tönung), `CIVibrance` (Dynamik), `CIToneCurve` (a five-point tone curve), and `CIHighlightShadowAdjust` (Schatten and Lichter). Neutral filters stay out of the chain. The panel shows the curve as a square with a quarter grid and the diagonal; the drawn curve is `CIToneCurve`'s own mapping of a gray ramp, the five points drag with the mouse (ordered in x with a small gap), and a double-click resets it. Below are sliders for Schatten (`inputShadowAmount`, −1…1), Lichter (`inputHighlightAmount − 1`, −0.7…0), Dynamik (`inputAmount`, −1…1), Wärme, and Tönung. Wärme and Tönung move `inputOrigI` and `inputOrigQ` against `CIFaceBalance`'s neutral skin tone (0.103905, 0.0176465): lower I warms, lower Q goes magenta, so + is warmer and + is magenta. Auto runs `autoAdjustmentFilters` (face detection on, red-eye off) on the frame on screen, the stored upscale or, when it is not cached yet, the original frame, and copies every parameter exactly, including `inputStrength`, `inputWarmth`, `inputRadius`, and `inputExtrapolate`; a filter Apple leaves out (no face, no face balance) stays neutral. The screen applies the chain live as a layer filter, to stills, playback, and the original alike, so moving a control computes nothing and leaves the frame cache alone. Core Animation runs layer filters color managed in a linear working space; Auto analyzes and an export renders the same way (the frame tagged with its BT.709 profile, rendered back into it), so preview, Auto, and export agree. A neutral grade keeps a ProRes export in the cache's flavor a plain copy of the cache. Zurücksetzen goes back to neutral, and a double-click on a value resets that one. Grades saved by older versions (brightness, contrast, gamma, saturation) load as neutral.

New stages (crop, deinterlacing, levels) go into `PreprocessConfig` (`Preprocess.swift`) and the frame map or the picture of `Film`; engine, export, and model only see the film the stages make. AppleScript: `decimate` (off, auto, cycle6, cycle5), `pipeline` (for example "30 → 25 fps · 44847 Bilder · 6er-Takt erkannt"), and `color grade` ("curve 0,0 0.25,0.25 0.5,0.5 0.75,0.75 1,1 shadows 0.1 highlights 0 vibrance 0.12 warmth -0.02 tint 0", neutral, or auto; names left out stay neutral, and `strength`, `facewarmth`, `radius`, and `extrapolate` appear only when they differ from the default).

### Settings and the frame cache

Whoodeo › Einstellungen… (⌘,) is one grouped form for the frame cache:

- **Format.** The ProRes flavor of new caches: 422 Proxy, 422 LT (the default), 422, 422 HQ, or 4444. Each archive records its flavor in its `meta.json`; archives from before the setting have none and stay ProRes 4444, readable and appendable as before. An export copies cached frames without encoding only when the archive's flavor is the export's (ProRes 4444 or ProRes 422 HQ) and the grade is neutral; otherwise it decodes and encodes again.
- **Speicherort.** Default `~/Library/Application Support/Whoodeo/Cache`. "Ändern…" picks a folder (a folder with other files gets a `Whoodeo Cache` folder inside; the cache folder carries a `.whoodeo-cache` marker, and pruning only ever touches folders named like a film digest). With a non-empty cache the app asks: "Verschieben" closes the open archives, copies every file, checks each by size, and deletes the old files only after everything arrived; "Nur wechseln" leaves the old files and starts empty. "Standard" goes back the same way. A custom folder whose drive is not connected is never created: nothing is stored or deleted, and the meter says "Cache-Ordner nicht erreichbar". The first time the app reads a cache on an external drive, macOS asks for access to removable volumes; later rebuilds keep the same designated requirement because Debug and Release are signed with the local "Whoodeo Local Signing" certificate in the login keychain, so macOS does not ask again. An ad-hoc signed build (`CODE_SIGN_IDENTITY = "-"`) asks after each rebuild. `Whoodeo --move-cache <folder>|default` does the same move from the command line while the app is not running.
- **Speicherlimit.** Off by default. At the limit the background fill pauses ("Cache-Limit erreicht – pausiert" under the meter) and nothing is deleted; frames looked at, played, or exported on purpose are still stored. Raising the limit, turning it off, or deleting a cache lets the fill go on.
- **Belegung.** The total and every film's cache with its size and "Löschen…", which deletes that film's frame caches (all models) and keeps the film in the list.

The keys live in the app's defaults (`cacheCodec`, `cacheDirectory`, `cacheLimitEnabled`, `cacheLimitGB`).

### MLX

A three-frame latent_up runs in the app through MLX, as one program: the per-frame front, SPyNet, the feature warp and fusion, the rest of latent_up with FiLM, and the vae2 decoder, with flexible sizes and no host step. `src/whoodeo/mlxport` is the Python port (`ops.py` for the PyTorch ops with the same conventions as `grid_sample` and `interpolate`, `model.py`), the reference for the Swift version in the app (`MLXAlign.swift`, mlx-swift). It needs the optional extra: `uv sync --extra mlx` (a plain `uv sync` drops mlx again). Building the app needs Xcode's Metal Toolchain once (`xcodebuild -downloadComponent MetalToolchain`), and the app is arm64 only.

    .venv/bin/python -m whoodeo.mlxport.convert ~/storinator/home/whoodeo/runs/<run>/checkpoints/<step> --app

writes `<run>-latent_up-12x128-step<step>-3f-hd-align-cond.mlxmodel` (a folder with `model.safetensors` and `config.json`) into the app's folder; the sidebar shows it in orange. SPyNet and the flow compute in fp32, latent_up and the decoder in fp16 (`WHOODEO_MLX_PRECISION=float32` for everything in fp32). The feature warp keeps its coordinates in fp32 and interpolates the fp16 features in fp16 (against fp32 interpolation 73.8 dB on a 720p frame, max 1.6/255; both are 73.0 dB from all-fp32). `python -m whoodeo.mlxport.check` compares the port stage by stage with PyTorch (flow, warp error, warped and fused features, code, decoder, image; `--fp16` as the app), `check_app` the app, `bench` times it.

Check of the feature-aligned port (24.11.2026, a fresh model with randomly perturbed weights, so no path is zero): Python MLX against PyTorch fp32 110–114 dB end to end on the CPU and 111 dB on the GPU in fp32 (flow 137–140 dB, fused features 133–136 dB); fp16 about 50 dB with these exaggerated weights; the app's Swift path is bit-identical to Python MLX. The pixel-aligned model before it (step 6500 of train-20261123-155857, MLX fp16) took 238 ms per frame at 384×288 and 1.63 s at 1280×720 in the app on the M1 Ultra; the feature-aligned one (step 6000 of train-20261124-100406) about 1.6 s at 1280×720.

Memory in the app (25.11.2026, 720p): the front (stem and the low-resolution blocks) runs one frame at a time, not as one batch of three, which drops the GPU peak from about 13 to under 8 GB with a bit-identical result. MLX keeps freed buffers in its cache for reuse; without a limit that reached about 37 GB, so the app sets `Memory.cacheLimit` to 12 GB (process footprint 37 → 14 GB). The front features of the last window are kept, keyed by film, source frame, size, and condition, so a run of consecutive frames computes the front only for the new frame; a seek or anything else just misses, and a frame's features do not depend on its neighbors, so a scene cut needs nothing. The result is bit-identical to computing all three. `--pace` also prints MLX's active, cache, and peak memory per frame.

Matroska files often carry an index of only some keyframes, sometimes just the first. When the index holds far fewer pictures than duration times frame rate, the app reads the packets instead, as it does without an index; before, such a file opened as a film of one frame.

### Shared database and the macOS trainer

The Xcode project (`macos/Whoodeo`) compiles a shared folder `WhoodeoCore` into both `Whoodeo.app` and the command-line tool `whoodeo-train`: the FFmpeg source reader, MLX model, VAE, pixels, log, cache identity, and `WhoodeoDB`. The Python trainer in this repo is also called `whoodeo-train`; the Swift tool is the macOS binary built from this project, not the venv command.

App and trainer share `~/Library/Application Support/Whoodeo/whoodeo.sqlite` (WAL mode, `libsqlite3`). Training films are stored with relative paths under a configurable `dataset_root` (default `/Volumes/Black Elem/whoodeo/train`). Checkpoints stay as `.mlxmodel` folders under `~/Library/Application Support/Whoodeo/Models/`. The database records the run family tree, generic metrics, and the frame-cache archives that belong to a checkpoint (`cache_files`: film digest plus `{modelKey}-gpu[-tag]` folder, so a later delete can remove the package, the optimizer, and those caches together). Existing cache folders are never renamed or deleted by the import; they are only associated. The title split is a stable 10 % validation holdout from the title name (FNV-1a). The earlier PyTorch trainer instead held out 10 % of the frames inside every film (two spans at 30 % and 70 %).

`whoodeo-train` trains `latent_up` in Swift/MLX on the same catalog. Everything except the thin CLI lives in `WhoodeoCore` (`TrainDataset`, `TrainLoss`, `TrainLoop`, `TrainConfig`), so a later training UI in the app can call the same code. Training is FP32 (optional `--dtype bf16` for compute); exported `.mlxmodel` packages are FP16, the format `Whoodeo.app` loads. Default seed is 42. Crops are per run (`--crop 384`, `--crop 0` for the full frame, must be 0 or a multiple of 32); the low crop is exactly half the size and at half the (even) master origin, for the 2× upscale. Input images stay in `[0, 1]`, the network predicts the normalized vae2 code `z = (μ - shift) * scale`, and image losses compare the unclamped decode `z / scale + shift` with the master, same as `train.py` and inference (`MLXAlign` + the frozen vae2). The official loss terms are latent L1 (1.0), Oklab (4.5, channels `[1, 1, 1]`), 5-scale MS-SSIM on Oklab L (scales with a side below 11 are dropped), and VGG19 `relu2_2` / `relu3_4` (0.230 / 0.155). Faces and scene cuts are not in this first Swift trainer; `codec_unknown` 0.2 is. Frozen assets (vae2 step 044750, VGG19 IMAGENET1K_V1 up to `relu3_4`, official SPyNet for a fresh run) live in `~/Library/Application Support/Whoodeo/Train/`. SPyNet stays frozen by default (`stopGradient` on the flow, weights outside the optimizer); `--train-spynet` lets it learn at `--spynet-lr-scale` times the main learning rate (default 0.1, or an absolute `--spynet-lr`). Those settings are stored in `runs.config_json` and shown in `whoodeo-train tree` and under Modelle. A trained SPyNet is part of the exported `.mlxmodel`, so `Whoodeo.app` loads it like any other checkpoint. New checkpoints are written to `Models/<run-name>/step-XXXXXX.mlxmodel` plus an optimizer sidecar. Each validation also writes the first four deterministic val samples as PNG triples (`sample-00-low.png`, `sample-00-pred.png`, `sample-00-orig.png`) plus `meta.json` under `Models/<run-name>/val/step-XXXXXX/`. The low frame is the middle input, scaled to the master size with high-quality interpolation so the three pictures line up 1:1. `whoodeo-train val-preview <ckpt-id>` writes the same set for any existing checkpoint. `--from <ckpt-id>` starts a fine-tune as a new branch (`parent_checkpoint_id`); `resume` continues a paused or aborted run from the last checkpoint and optimizer state. Training opens the catalog files with `source_open_fast` (no packet timeline scan; the frame time is `start_time` plus the stream frame rate, with a backward keyframe seek and forward decode, and consecutive `t-1, t, t+1` stay sequential). The app and inference still use `source_open` with the full timeline. By default four prefetch workers fill a short batch queue (`--workers 4`, `--workers 0` for the old synchronous path); each worker has its own FFmpeg handles. `SIGINT`/`SIGTERM` finish the current step, write a pause checkpoint, and set `runs.status = paused`. The first `SIGINT` prints that it will stop after the step; a second `Ctrl+C` exits immediately with status 130. A checkpoint can be requested without stopping the run: `whoodeo-train checkpoint now`, `SIGUSR1`, or the button Checkpoint jetzt unter Modelle. After the current step a visible `.mlxmodel` is written and training continues. A running run can also be stopped from Modelle (Lauf stoppen) or with `whoodeo-train stop [run-id|name]`: after the current step the trainer writes a pause checkpoint, sets `runs.status = paused`, and exits, same as the first `SIGINT`. The request lives in the `settings` row `stop_request.<run_id>`, analogous to `checkpoint_request.<run_id>`. A paused, finished, failed, or imported run can be deleted there (Lauf löschen) after a confirmation; that removes the run, its checkpoints, optimizer files, associated caches, and any stored val preview images under `Models/<run>/val/`. Child runs must be deleted first. The app lists every checkpoint in the tree (`visible_in_app` remains in the database for older tools, but the app no longer filters or shows a visibility switch).

    whoodeo-train start --name align64-test --channels 64 --crop 256 --batch 2 --grad-accum 2 --steps 4 --save-every 4 --val-every 4
    whoodeo-train start --name align64-ft --from <ckpt-id> --crop 384 --steps 2 --save-every 2
    whoodeo-train start --name align64-spy --from <ckpt-id> --train-spynet --spynet-lr-scale 0.1 --steps 2 --save-every 2
    whoodeo-train resume align64-test --steps 4
    whoodeo-train tree
    whoodeo-train models
    whoodeo-train checkpoint hide <ckpt-id>
    whoodeo-train checkpoint show <ckpt-id>
    whoodeo-train checkpoint delete <ckpt-id> --yes
    whoodeo-train checkpoint now
    whoodeo-train val-preview <ckpt-id>
    whoodeo-train start --w-oklab 0 --w-ms-ssim 0 --w-vgg 0 --steps 2000     # latent only, no decode, auto name
    whoodeo-train start --from <ckpt-id> --w-latent 0 --w-oklab 0 --w-ms-ssim 0  # VGG only
    whoodeo-train resume <run> --steps 500 --lr 5e-5 --w-vgg 2

Every training parameter is declared once in `WhoodeoCore/TrainParameterSpec.swift`: key in `runs.config_json`, CLI flag, section, label, help text, type, default, range or options, and scope. `whoodeo-train --help` and the app's start sheet are generated from it, and the same text parser and `TrainConfig.validated()` check both. Sections: Architektur (`--channels`, `--encoder-blocks`, `--blocks`, `--dtype`; scope `newOnly`), Optimierer & Schedule (`--steps`, `--lr`, `--schedule`, `--batch`, `--grad-accum`, `--train-spynet`/`--no-train-spynet`, `--spynet-lr-scale`), Loss-Gewichte (`--w-latent` 1, `--w-oklab` 4.5, `--w-ms-ssim` 1, `--w-vgg` 1 as a factor on the VGG layer weights), and Daten & Laufzeit (`--crop`, `--rungs`, `--presets`, `--codec-unknown`, `--workers`, `--seed`, `--val-every`, `--val-count`, `--save-every`, `--log-every`, `--hidden`/`--no-hidden`). Architecture flags are rejected with `--from` and `resume`; there they come from the parent run or the run itself. All other flags also work with `resume`, which writes the changed config back to the run. Older runs without the newer keys (`w_*`, `log_every`) load with the defaults. A loss weight of 0 switches the term off: it is not computed and not logged in training (validation still reports Oklab and MS-SSIM for PSNR/SSIM, VGG only when it is on). With `--w-vgg 0` VGG19 is not loaded; with Oklab, MS-SSIM and VGG all 0 the training step skips the VAE decode (the log says so and counts step decodes at the end), and with `--w-latent 0` it skips the target encode. At least one weight must be above 0. `start` without `--name` uses `train-YYYYMMDD-HHMMSS`; an existing name is refused.

Modelle can start training too: the + button in the toolbar (Neuer Lauf), Fine-Tune… in a checkpoint's detail header, and Fortsetzen… in the header of a paused or finished run. The sheet is built from `TrainParameterSpec`; architecture is shown locked for fine-tune and resume, loss terms have a switch each (off = weight 0) and hints when the decode or VGG drops out, and the start button stays disabled while the CLI validation would fail. The app runs the `whoodeo-train` next to `Whoodeo.app` (`build/Release`, or inside the bundle) with `posix_spawn` in its own session, so quitting the app does not stop the training; stdout and stderr are appended to `Models/<run-name>/train.log` (Log öffnen in the run header). The headers show the run and checkpoint ids (`#4`) for the CLI. `run delete` also removes `train.log`. AppleScript: `main pane` (films/models), `trainer path`, and `start training "start --steps 100 --w-vgg 0"` (same detached launch, returns the run name).

`Whoodeo.app` switches the main window between Filme and Modelle (toolbar segmented control, or Darstellung → Filme / Modelle, shortcuts ⌘1 and ⌘2). The old sidebar model list is gone. In Filme a compact line shows the active model and, if one is set, the reference model; a click there opens Modelle. The reference model is what a click in the preview shows; set it with ⌥-click on a checkpoint, or with Als Referenz / Referenz entfernen in the tree, the context menu or the detail card, just like the old option-click in the model list. A normal click in the tree only selects the run or checkpoint for the detail pane; the tree itself stays quiet (names, status, step, and Aktiv / Referenz badges). The active model changes only through the explicit Aktivieren button in the right-hand detail header, next to Als Referenz / Referenz entfernen and Checkpoint löschen (or stays a non-clickable Aktiv badge when that checkpoint is already active). ⌥-click in the tree still sets the reference model, and the same actions remain in the row context menu. Modelle shows the run family tree with every checkpoint and those badges. The right side shows run facts, the checkpoint and run actions, live loss charts (Swift Charts) from the `metrics` table, a Validierungsbilder section (step and sample picker, side-by-side input / model / original or a large single view, with pixel-sharp zoom and pan: pinch, scroll, and 100 % / 200 % / 400 % / Einpassen; zoom and the visible crop stay in sync across the three pictures), and — when a run is selected — Lauf stoppen for a running trainer or a guarded Lauf löschen for any other status. If a run has no val pictures yet, the section says so and points to the next val step or `whoodeo-train val-preview <ckpt-id>`. The tree refreshes every few seconds and when the database WAL changes, but only while Modelle is open. Imported runs without metrics show a short note instead of an empty chart.

`whoodeo-train tree` (also `status`) prints the runs, parent→child tree, crops, SPyNet (frozen or trained with its LR scale), visibility, last metrics, and counts. `whoodeo-train models` lists the models the app would show. There is no `db sync` command: the first open of the database imports `catalog.json` once, adopts `.mlxmodel` packages (including nested `Models/<run>/` folders) that are not in the database yet, and associates existing caches under the app's cache folder.

    xcodebuild -project Whoodeo.xcodeproj -scheme whoodeo-train -configuration Release SYMROOT=$PWD/build build

# The loss and the configs

The loss is the sum of `weight` times each raw term under `loss.terms`. A weight is a plain multiplier: nothing is normalized or calibrated on the network, and the weights need not add up to anything. `pixel` is L1 or MSE, `kind: l1` or `kind: mse`. `vgg` has no term weight; `layers` maps each VGG19 layer to its weight on the L1 of that layer. `color`, `oklab`, and `ms_ssim` are for `vae2` and `latent_up`, `mu_l2` and `scale_eq` for `vae2` only, `latent` for `latent_up` only. A vae2 `gan` term adds as described with the vae2. TensorBoard groups tags by their first segment, so `train/` and `val/` hold the overview and the details sit in groups that collapse on their own. `train/` has `loss`, `lr`, and each weighted term; `val/` has `loss`, `psnr` (which compares across loss weights), and each weighted term. The unweighted values are under `train_raw/` and `val_raw/` (VGG as `vgg_<layer>`, the vae2 side terms and GAN weight in `train_raw/`). Latent statistics are under `train_latent/` and `val_latent/`, with the histogram `val_latent/mu` and the text table `val_latent/channels`. The vae2 robustness PSNRs are under `val_noise/`, the latent_up reference PSNRs under `val_ref/`, and the three-frame neighbor numbers under `train_neighbor/` and `val_neighbor/`. A `--resume` needs the same loss as the checkpoint.

- `configs/vae2g-spark.yaml`: the current vae2 recipe on the DGX Spark (relu, oklab and ms_ssim beside VGG, latent noise, `mu_l2`, `scale_eq`), with face-weighted image terms, `faces: {weight: 3.0, score: 0.6, expand: 1.2, feather: 0.1}`.
- `configs/vae2-gan.yaml`: the GAN fine-tune of a vae2 decoder, encoder frozen, `--model <run>/latest`.
- `configs/latent-up-3f-128-falign-spark.yaml`: the width-128 FiLM latent_up with three input frames and feature alignment inside the model (frozen SPyNet, warped low-resolution features, 3×3 fusion), scene cuts, and the weight average, fresh from zero on spark-03; data x264-crf18–24 and mpeg4-q04/q06 (no mpeg4-q08, no x264-crf26).
- `configs/latent-up-3f-128-falign-ft-spark.yaml`: fine-tune branch of that run on spark-04 (`--model` checkpoint 002500 of train-20261124-100406), same recipe except latent L1 weight 0.25 instead of 1.0; `publish_latest: false`, so the main run stays `runs/latest`.
